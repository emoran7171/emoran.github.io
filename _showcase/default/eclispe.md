    <h3>National Eclipse Ballooning Project (NEBP)  </h3>
    <p> As a member of the University of Pittsburgh's National Eclipse Ballooning Project (NEBP) team,  I spent nights outside in rural Texas, attaching radiosondes to weather balloons to collect radio observations of the atmosphere for NASA during the 2023 annular and 2024 total solar eclipses. Unlike other NEBP teams, we were also investigating shadow bands: wavy, moving bands of shadows unique to eclipses, first observed in the 9th century. The leading hypothesis claims the bands are caused by refraction from atmospheric turbulence, but a new hypothesis suggests that they may be the result of the moon functioning as a slit as it covers the sun. To test this theory, we launched a high-altitude balloon to test whether shadow bands appear outside the atmosphere; you can read about our results in <a href="https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2026arXiv260113335D/abstract">Deskevich et al. 2026</a>. </p>
