    <h1>SDSS Machine Learning Photometric Redshift Estimation/h1> 
    <p>With Professors Brett Andrews and Jeffrey Newman at the University of Pittsburgh, I completed my primary undergraduate research project studying Machine Learning (ML) methods for photometric red shift (photo-z) estimation. Deep learning networks are often referred to as black boxes: they work with millions of parameters and complex structures, making their processes difficult to interrupt, and I have spent my past two years of undergrad shining a light into that particular black box. By utilizing a variety of statistical and ML methods (Spearman and Pearson correlations, random forests, self-organizing maps, UMAP), I discovered that non-deep-learning methods exhibit a color-dependent attenuation bias when restricted subsets are analyzed that are not readily apparent in the global data set; deep learning methods do not exhibit the same problem. Attenuation bias is a common ML bias due to errors in the input. We hypothesize that deep learning algorithms using images as inputs extract more pixel-level information than classical ML methods that use photometry as input. My first author paper on the subject can be read [here.](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2026ApJ...997...36M/abstract)</p>
